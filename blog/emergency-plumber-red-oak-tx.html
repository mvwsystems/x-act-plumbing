First 60 Seconds: Stop the Water

Most plumbing emergencies get dramatically worse in the minutes before a plumber arrives, so your first job is to cut the flow. For a leaking toilet or faucet, turn the small oval shut-off valve on the supply line below the fixture clockwise. For a burst pipe, a failed water heater, or a leak you cannot locate, go straight to your main shut-off.

  • Main shut-off inside: Often near where the line enters the house — in the garage, a utility closet, or under a sink on newer Red Oak builds.
  • Main shut-off outside: At the water meter box near the street. You may need a meter key or a wrench to turn the valve a quarter turn.
  • Water heater: Turn off the cold-water supply valve on top of the tank, and shut off the gas or breaker before it runs dry.
Pro tip: Find and label your main shut-off valve today, while it is calm. In an actual emergency at 2 a.m., knowing exactly where it is — and having a meter key in the garage — can save thousands in water damage.

What Counts as a Real Plumbing Emergency

Not every plumbing problem needs an after-hours visit, and a straight answer saves you money. Call for emergency service when the problem is actively causing damage or making the home unsafe. It can usually wait for a normal appointment when the issue is contained and you have another fixture to use.

Who You Should Actually Call in Red Oak

The plumber you call in an emergency should meet three tests: licensed in Texas, local enough to arrive quickly, and willing to inspect before quoting. Texas requires plumbers to be licensed through the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners, and any legitimate company operates under a Responsible Master Plumber — so ask for that license, not just a business name. What Emergency Plumbing Costs in Red Oak

Emergency and after-hours plumbing generally costs more than a scheduled weekday visit because of the trip and the timing, not because the work is different. Most reputable companies charge a service or diagnostic fee to come out, then quote the repair up front once they see the problem. Be cautious of anyone who quotes a firm repair price over the phone without seeing the failure — a burst pipe behind a wall and a burst pipe under a slab are very different jobs.

Red Oak's Most Common Emergencies

The failures we respond to most often in Red Oak follow the seasons and the local building stock.

  • Winter pipe bursts. North Texas swings hard between mild and hard freezes. Uninsulated pipes in attics, exterior walls, and garages crack when water freezes and expands — and the flood often shows up when things thaw. Drip your faucets and open cabinet doors on freeze nights.
  • Slab leaks. Many Red Oak and Ellis County homes sit on concrete slabs over expansive clay soil that shifts with drought and rain. That movement stresses the lines under the foundation. If you hear running water with everything off, or see a warm spot on the floor, read our slab leak guide and call quickly.
  • Sewer backups. Mature trees send roots into older clay sewer laterals, and grease buildup finishes the job. When it backs up into the lowest drains, it is an emergency.
  • Water heater failures. Tanks tend to fail on the coldest mornings, when demand is highest and a leaking tank can flood a closet fast.
Pro tip: Before a hard freeze, disconnect and drain garden hoses, and cover outdoor spigots with an insulated cover. A frozen hose bib is one of the most common — and most preventable — burst-pipe calls we get every winter in Red Oak.
