The Definitive Answer

Does Your Drain System
Hold Water?

A hydrostatic test is the industry-standard way to verify the sewer and drain lines under a slab foundation. We plug the main line at the cleanout, fill the system with water to slab level, and watch the level. If it holds, your drains are sound. If it drops, water is escaping under your foundation — and in North Texas expansive clay, that moisture is exactly what swells the soil and moves foundations.

When a test fails, we don't just hand you bad news. We isolate the leaking section with camera inspection and isolation testing, then lay out your repair options — targeted spot repair versus re-route — with honest pricing before any work begins. If the concern started as a suspected slab leak on the water side, our leak detection service covers pressurized lines the same way.

Common Questions

Hydrostatic Testing FAQ

What is a hydrostatic test?

A hydrostatic test checks the sewer and drain system under your home for leaks. We plug the main sewer line at the cleanout, fill the drain system with water to slab level, and watch the water level. If it holds, the system is sound. If it drops, there is a leak under the slab — and we can isolate and locate it.

Do I need one when buying or selling a home in Texas?

It is one of the most common inspection follow-ups in North Texas real estate. Home inspectors frequently recommend a hydrostatic test on slab-foundation homes — especially older ones with cast iron drains — and buyers often request one during the option period. A licensed plumber must perform it.

Can a hydrostatic test damage my pipes?

A properly performed test fills the system to slab level only — the same pressure your drains see in normal use. On older cast iron systems we inspect first and test carefully. Testing performed by an unlicensed handyman is where horror stories come from; X-Act tests are performed by a licensed plumber.

What happens if my home fails the test?

A failed test tells you the drain system is leaking — the next step is isolating which section. We use camera inspection and isolation testing to pinpoint the leak, then give you honest repair options with clear pricing, from targeted spot repair to a re-route.
